# Evidence Architecture Reference

Read this when building the claim-evidence matrix, deciding where proof goes on a specific site type, writing disclosure and recency copy, or reviewing testimonials and logos.

## 1. Claim types and the evidence each needs

| Claim type | Example | Minimum evidence | Better evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | "We are a registered charity" | Legal name and registration number | Link to the regulator's public register entry |
| Accreditation | "Accredited programme" | Issuing body and scope | Accreditation number and verification link, valid-until date |
| Scale | "500 clients" | Dated figure and counting method | Figure with "as of" date, owner, and what counts as a client |
| Outcome | "92% of graduates employed" | Cohort, timeframe, method, response rate | Third-party or audited report link |
| Expertise | "Expert team" | Named people with roles | Bios with credentials, publications, verifiable profiles |
| Relationship | Client logo | Documented relationship and permission | Case study with named contact and date |
| Safety or compliance | "Fully certified building" | Certificate name and issuer | Certificate number, date, and document |
| Superlative | "Leading provider" | Named third-party ranking and date | Otherwise rewrite to a specific, checkable statement |

## 2. Placement patterns by site type

Education:
- Fees, funding, accreditation and entry requirements on each programme page, beside the apply action.
- Outcome statistics with cohort and method, not on the homepage alone.

Real estate and buildings:
- Developer identity, permits or registration, completion status, and handover date near the enquiry form.
- Label renderings and sample units as illustrative; show real photos when available with date.

Nonprofit:
- Registration, how donations are used with a dated breakdown, and the latest annual report beside the donate step.
- Impact numbers dated and sourced; beneficiary stories with consent noted.

Corporate and B2B:
- Case studies with named clients (with permission), dates and scope beside the contact sales action.
- Security or compliance attestations named with scope and period; link to trust centre if it exists.

High-consideration ecommerce:
- Returns, warranty and total cost before commitment, not only in the footer.
- Reviews with source, verification status and sort options including most recent and lowest rated.

## 3. Disclosure patterns

Material connection near the endorsement, same visual weight as the endorsement text:
- "Paid partnership with [Brand]."
- "[Name] received the product free for review."
- "[Name] is an employee of [Company]."

Atypical results:
- Avoid outcome testimonials unless you can state what typical customers experience. "Results vary" alone is not an adequate qualifier under FTC guidance; verify current guidance.

Reviews:
- State where reviews come from, whether they are verified purchases, and whether any are filtered and why.
- Do not hide or downrank negative reviews while presenting the rest as representative.

## 4. Recency copy

- "Fees last reviewed 12 March 2026 by Admissions."
- "Figures as of 31 December 2025. Next update after annual report."
- "Archived page: information may be out of date. See current [programme page]."
Avoid "Updated recently" or a date that auto-refreshes on every deploy; it is a false recency signal.

## 5. Logo and testimonial audit questions

- Is there a contract, invoice, membership record or written permission?
- Is the relationship still active? If not, is it labelled past ("worked with, 2021 to 2023") or removed?
- Does the label describe the relationship accurately (client, partner, member, featured in)?
- Is the testimonial from a real, identifiable person who consented to its use and wording?
- Was it edited? Edits must not change meaning.
- Is any material connection disclosed?

## 6. Worked example (before and after, education programme page)

Before:

> Our award-winning, internationally accredited MBA is trusted by leading companies. 98% of graduates get promoted. "Life-changing!" says Anna K.
> Logos: Google, Microsoft, Unilever.
> Apply now.

Problems: unnamed award, no accreditor, logos without stated relationship, outcome statistic with no cohort or method, testimonial unverifiable, fees absent at decision point.

After:

> Accredited by [Accreditor name], accreditation no. [number], valid to [date]. [Verify on accreditor's register]
> 2024 graduate survey: 71 of 88 respondents (response rate stated) reported a promotion or new role within 12 months. Method and full report.
> Employers of 2023 to 2024 graduates who agreed to be listed: [logos, labelled "Graduate employers"].
> "The finance module changed how I run my team." Anna Kowalski, Operations Lead, class of 2024. (Alumna; no payment received.)
> Fees: [amount] total, instalment options, refund policy. Last reviewed [date] by Admissions.
> Apply now. Questions? Admissions replies within 2 working days: [email], [phone].

## 7. Error and polish as credibility

Typos, broken links, dead contact forms and placeholder text are credibility failures under the Stanford guidelines, not cosmetic issues. Include a link and form check in the trust audit.
