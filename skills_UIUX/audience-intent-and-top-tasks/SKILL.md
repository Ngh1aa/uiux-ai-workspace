# Audience Intent & Top Tasks

## Principle
A website is not for an abstract "target audience". It serves people in specific situations trying to make progress.

`audience → trigger/context → need → top task → question → evidence → action → success`

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer, in order:
1. direct user research and observed behavior;
2. analytics, search logs, CRM/call-centre/support data;
3. sales/admissions/service-team evidence;
4. credible market/domain research;
5. stakeholder input;
6. assumptions clearly labelled as assumptions.

Never turn stakeholder opinion into a fake user finding.

## Workflow
### 1. Separate audiences by meaningful behavior
Segment only when groups differ in task, context, evidence needs, constraints or decision process. Avoid decorative personas based only on age/job title.

### 2. Capture visit triggers
For every meaningful audience record:
- what happened before the visit;
- what outcome they want;
- urgency/risk level;
- prior knowledge;
- device/channel constraints;
- whether another person influences the decision.

### 3. Identify top tasks
Rank tasks by user importance and business consequence. A top task is something the user came to accomplish, not a page name.

Bad: `Read About Us`.
Better: `Decide whether this organization is credible enough to contact`.

### 4. Build question/evidence map
For each task define:
- user question;
- answer required;
- proof/evidence required;
- likely objection;
- next action;
- success signal.

### 5. Resolve business/user tension
Business objectives may influence prioritization, but do not hide or distort critical user information to force conversion.

## Required artifact
Create/update `docs/audience-intent.md` with:
- audience groups;
- triggers and contexts;
- top-task ranking;
- question/evidence/action matrix;
- evidence vs assumptions;
- implications for homepage, IA and conversion.

## Gate
Before major homepage/IA work, the team should be able to answer:
- Who is this experience for?
- Why are they here now?
- What are the 3–5 most important things they need to do or decide?
- What evidence will give them confidence?
- What outcome means success for them?

## Anti-patterns
- One generic persona for every user.
- Page names masquerading as user tasks.
- Prioritizing internal departments over user intent.
- Treating all visitors as homepage-first.
- Inventing research findings to justify a preferred design.
